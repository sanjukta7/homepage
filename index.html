Most recently, I had started Atlas Discovery, an AI-native pharma company, which went through Y Combinator's summer batch. Prior to this, I spent some months at Los Alamos National Lab studying architectures aimed at sample-efficient learning. And started a PhD at the University of Bristol in AI.

I spent time evaluating and benchmarking agents and models on clinical tasks. In biology, a model can look strong on the data it was built around and still fail on a new patient, a new assay, or a harder question.

Another interesting line of work came from asking whether pre-clinical data can predict who will respond to a drug in a clinical trial, and who will not. Here are a few examples: 1, 2, 3, 4.

Earlier, I worked on architectures aimed at sample-efficient learning, including experiments with Modern Hopfield Networks, motivated by how much data current language models need. My Master's by Research at the University of Edinburgh, with Prof. Antonio Vergari, was on compiling and approximating probabilistic circuits from Markov random fields.
